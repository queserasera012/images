○ PickOnly for a measured shortfall in accuracy

Caching
○ PickFixed first, changing last

○ PickGrounding (retrieval, tools, citations)

Points not to drop

  • Least privilege: remove the tools that are not used (before adding logs or confirmations)
  • Authorization happens at tool execution, with the calling user’s rights. The user’s role is injected on the server side (do not trust what is declared)
  • An action with side effects → deterministic authorization before execution. An output filter does not protect it
  • Idempotency keys prevent double execution (a double refund through a retry)
  • Pass personal data as only the fields needed, by reference ID. ZDR and tenant isolation go in at the start of design

Using an LLM as the judge
○ PickCalibrate on human labels, limit the verdicts (pass/fail), use another model family, pin the version
× AvoidUsing it uncalibrated (worse than none)
A change with low traffic or no tolerance for error
○ PickA shadow test (no effect on production)
× AvoidA/B in production
Quality fell
○ PickInput → retrieval → prompt → model → post-processing, one at a time
× AvoidChanging everything together
Estimating cost
○ PickProduction volume and the distribution. Latency is p95
× AvoidThe POC bill, an average
Checking quality
○ PickAn evaluation suite
× AvoidManual spot checks
Monitoring production
○ PickEvery stage by trace ID, scheduled quality measurement, drift detection, PII masking
× AvoidError rate and latency only

Governance, safety and risk (14%)

Safety is a stack of layers (do not rely on one)

InputChecks, delimiters
→
ModelSystem prompt, policy
→
ActionDeterministic authorization, least privilege
→
OutputChecks, PII
→
PeopleRouted by severity × confidence
A guardrail went down
○ PickFail closed (close explicitly)
× AvoidCarrying on with everything passing
Human review
○ PickTiered by severity and confidence
× AvoidEverything (consent fatigue) / nothing
Compliance
○ PickAll the way to controls, owners and evidence artifacts
× AvoidStopping at choosing a conforming route
Telling the regulations apart
○ PickGDPR = deletion, residency, minimization / HIPAA = BAA, PHI / FedRAMP = authorized environment
× AvoidDealing with it later
Bias
○ PickMeasure with control data. For decisions with a large effect on people, a final human decision plus explainability
× AvoidInstructing it to “be fair”

Stakeholders and lifecycle (14%)

A vague request (“fast”, “safe”)
○ PickTranslate it into a constraint and confirm (p95 within 3 seconds, and so on)
× AvoidMaking it a requirement as it stands
The first meeting
○ PickA design only after the four questions are finished
× AvoidShowing a draft right away
Explaining a design
○ PickWhat is gained, what is given up, the cost of reversal. Tell the limits too
× AvoidThe conclusion and what is possible only
Recording a decision
○ PickAn ADR (decision, rejected options, reasons)
× AvoidA diagram alone
Reporting outcomes
○ PickA before-and-after comparison of business metrics, with controls
× AvoidRequest counts, latency and error rate alone
SLA
○ PickAgreed in measurable form, including the response when exceeded
× Avoid“High quality”
Handoff
○ PickDocuments plus operating procedures plus an owner for monitoring
× AvoidOnly handing over the code

Developer productivity and operational support (7%)

The minimum

  • Roll out from champions, a few people at a time. Everyone at once is wrong
  • For operational problems, leave it in a runbook and teach the team (do not fix it yourself and stop there)
  • The organization’s floor is managed settings (top priority, cannot be overridden); team know-how goes in CLAUDE.md, Skills and plugins
  • Permission modes: unattended is dontAsk plus an allowlist. bypassPermissions is for isolated environments only

Last check (exam day)

  • The four things to decide before building (what, shape, base, touchpoints)
  • The order of the five factors (predictability → cost of error → observability → latency → cost)
  • Claude’s four properties and how to make up for them
  • The four questions of discovery. A design comes after
  • A trade-off = gain, give up, cost of reversal
  • Single call → workflow → agent. Rules in code, state through tools
  • Start with Sonnet and move by evaluation. Extended thinking only for a measured shortfall
  • When to use tool use, MCP or agent-to-agent, and all-at-once or progressive
  • The three execution platforms and where the data sits
  • Least privilege, authorization at execution, roles on the server side, idempotency
  • Evaluation first. The judge: calibrated, another model, pinned. Shadow → canary
  • Diagnose one at a time. Estimates use the distribution and p95
  • Layers, fail closed, HITL by severity × confidence
